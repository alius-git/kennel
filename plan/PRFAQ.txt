Press Release
Introducing Kennel A ready-to-use lab environment that gets you testing a Go2 quadruped controller in minutes—not days.
New York — July 20, 2026 — Today we announce Kennel, a ready-to-use virtual lab for researchers, students, and robotics teams who want to evaluate and practice with a modern Go2 walking controller without spending their first week fighting software setup.
Quadruped control stacks are powerful, but getting a clean environment running is often the real barrier. Teams lose time installing dependencies, aligning versions, and rebuilding machines before they ever see a simulated robot walk or learn how the controller behaves. Kennel removes that barrier. It delivers a prepared workspace where the controller experience is already waiting: open the lab, launch the simulation path, and start testing.
Kennel lives as a portable virtual machine (VM) that runs on a standard lab or personal computer. Users import the appliance, follow a short first-run checklist, and within minutes can exercise the controller in simulation changing gaits, sending velocity commands, and observing how the stack responds. The product is designed for learning, validation, demos, and onboarding.
Today, many teams face a gap: research controllers exist, documentation exists, but a shared “it just works” starting point does not. One person’s laptop works; another’s does not. Course labs and research groups repeat the same setup pain. Kennel closes that gap by making the first successful controller session the default outcome, not a heroic one.
“I used to budget a full week just to get the stack compiling before I could try a single trot in simulation. With Kennel, I was walking the simulated Go2 the same afternoon I started—and I finally spent my time on the controller, not on installation.” — Alex Rivera, Graduate Researcher in Legged Robotics
Kennel will be available first for lab and classroom use, with a clear path for teams that later want to take validated controller workflows toward real hardware on a dedicated robot computer. Early access and updates will be announced through the project channels below.
Customer FAQ (General Public)
1. What is Kennel, and who is it for?
Kennel is a ready-to-use virtual lab designed for students, researchers, and robotics teams to test a Go2 quadruped controller. Instead of spending days on fragile installations, you receive a prepared virtual machine. In your very first session, you can bring up a simulated Go2, stand the robot, start the walking controller, and drive basic motion and gait changes. It provides a reliable, shared starting point for learning, debugging, and validating controller behavior.
2. Why use a virtual machine instead of just following installation documentation?
Kennel solves the "setup tax." Documentation tells you how to build a lab from scratch, which often leads to mismatched dependencies and broken builds across different laptops. A VM provides a shared, resettable experience where the intended environment is already assembled. It allows a lab or classroom to hand out the exact same working baseline to every new teammate, making the "first successful test session" the product, rather than an afterthought.
3. Is Kennel meant to control a real Go2 robot out of the box?
No. Kennel’s primary promise is a ready test lab focused on simulation first. It does not replace a dedicated robot-side computer (like a Jetson) and is not advertised as a production controller for field robots. Real-robot use is an optional later step on suitable hardware, once the team safely understands the controller in simulation. Kennel is the practice yard that gets you ready.
Internal FAQ (Stakeholders)
1. Why are we building a VM lab instead of a deployable robot controller?
Reducing the "time-to-first-successful-test" drastically increases adoption, teaching impact, and research throughput. A VM shines at reproducibility, onboarding, and safe testing. Attempting to sell a VM as a deployed production controller would overclaim its capabilities and create safety and performance risks (e.g., virtualization disrupting hard real-time performance). Framing it strictly as a test appliance is defensible, safe, and highly compelling for our target audience.
2. What is the non-negotiable customer experience, and what is out of scope for v1?
The non-negotiable experience: A new user must be able to reach a walking, simulated Go2 in about five minutes after the appliance is imported and started—bypassing the multi-day dependency hunt. Out of scope for v1: Production claims for real-robot control from inside the VM, guaranteeing hard real-time performance in virtualization, promising it "works on every Jetson forever," or replacing the upstream research stack (the research codebase remains the source of truth; Kennel is just the ready front door).
3. How will we measure success, and what are the primary risks?
Success metrics: Within 90 days, multiple users should be able to start cold and complete the simulation walkthrough without expert hand-holding. Within 12 months, Kennel should be formally integrated into at least one course or lab onboarding path, with documented bridges from "validated in Kennel" to "run on dedicated hardware." Risks & Mitigations: The main risks are users overpromising real-robot control, skipping simulation to unsafely attempt hardware runs, or environment drift. We mitigate these through strict product framing ("test lab"), robust version pinning, and enforcing a simulation-first safety checklist before providing any hardware notes.
