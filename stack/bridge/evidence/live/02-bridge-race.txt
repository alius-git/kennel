2026-09-07 (UTC)
guest kennel-vm at 192.168.122.32, stack up, rosbridge 2.0.7 in the pinned image.

THE QUESTION. kennel-bridge.sh `start` waits for TWO signals -- /rosbridge_websocket
in `ros2 node list` AND :9090 bound in `ss` -- and plan/teleop-joystick.md D.3 §8
asks for the observation that justifies waiting for both instead of one.

READ THAT CAREFULLY, BECAUSE IT IS WRONG. It says the node arrives first and the
port a fraction of a second later, every single time. That is an artefact of the
instrument: the two probes cost very different amounts -- `ss` on the guest is a
few milliseconds, `ros2 node list` inside the container is ~0.9 s -- and they
share one polling loop, so the cheap probe only ever runs in the gaps the
expensive one leaves. The port cannot be OBSERVED before the node check returns,
so it can never be REPORTED first, whatever the stack actually did.

THE ANSWER, AND WHY THE WAIT IS BOTH. The port is bound FIRST in every start, by
0.10 to 2.18 seconds. So the cheap signal is the EARLY one: a `start` that
returned as soon as :9090 answered would hand the operator a URL up to 2.2 s
before /rosbridge_websocket was in the ROS graph -- a socket that accepts a
WebSocket and then cannot resolve interfaces/msg/QuadControlTarget, because the
ROS side of the bridge is not up yet. That is exactly the shape of #52: a gate
that watched one signal and returned while another was still arriving.

Iteration 5 is the one that matters. Nine starts put the node 0.10-0.55 s behind
the port; one put it 2.18 s behind. A wait tuned to the median would have been
wrong on that start, and nothing about the median predicts it.

The reverse is also true and is why the node check stays: a node in the graph
with no socket bound is a bridge no browser can reach. Neither signal implies
the other, and this is what "readiness is observed, twice" means.
